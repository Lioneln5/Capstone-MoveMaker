The supported decision
What should a club investigate before extending an existing player?

Separate evidence on realized contribution, movement, public-value downside, contract commitment, and historical peers—never one “extend” or “do not extend” score.

The final decision remains with the sporting director, informed by medical, tactical, legal, scouting, squad-planning, and internal financial context that public data cannot observe.

01 · Analytical journey

Three scopes. Two evidence-led pivots.

The project became more useful by making a smaller claim. Each pivot followed a failed or unsupported hypothesis rather than a change in presentation alone.

01 · Original hypothesis

Compatibility + transfer ROI

Profile player–player and player–club relationships, then combine sporting fit with financial data to estimate a signing's return. Rich feature requirements reduced the complete cohort to 63 cases, and broader tests did not recover a stable universal signal.

Rejected
02 · First pivot

Modular transfer risks

Opportunity, movement, public-value downside, fees, and capital risk were tested separately. Individual signals existed, but the mechanisms answered different questions and could not defensibly become one success or ROI number.

Risk, not fit
03 · Final scope

Extension-risk case study

Public wage and extension histories made one concrete decision observable: evaluating an existing player at the incumbent club. Predictors were restricted to the extension date and outcomes were measured afterward.

Frozen research record
02 · Final evidence

What survived—and what did not.

Predictive performance was only one gate. A result also needed a coherent football meaning, chronological consistency, subgroup reliability, and honest holdout governance.

Qualified research signal4 / 4 origins

Sustained realized contribution

Did the player reach at least 25% of the extension club's opportunity share in each exact post-signing year? The repaired target requires evidence in both years and sufficient club-schedule coverage. This is the contribution component of Meaningful Retention; the joint endpoint remains research only.

AUC 0.762
Positive baseline-relative skill in every origin; 6 of 9 core league/position groups passed the strict reliability gate.
Primary research horizon12 months

Public-value downside

Did the public market-value estimate decline by at least 25%? The 12-month model retained useful development evidence, but public value is not a transfer fee, accounting valuation, or realized loss.

AUC 0.760
Positive in 4 of 4 origins; 8 of 9 broad groups supported. No pristine untouched final cohort remains.

Temporary Displacement Risk

A temporary first movement, such as a loan, is modeled separately from permanent separation. The question is football-coherent, but later player-disjoint final evidence is not yet mature enough.

04 · Research standard

Why the model is no longer live.

The original web prototype was deployed successfully. After the presentation, an adversarial audit found that several polished outputs had weaker target definitions, subgroup support, or final-test governance than their presentation implied.

01

Meaning before performance

Loans and permanent exits were separated; realized involvement was distinguished from talent or tactical fit; incomplete Year-1/Year-2 evidence was no longer accepted.

02

Reliability before averages

A strong pooled score could not conceal a league or position failure. The annual-wage candidate was rejected after its Ligue 1 range coverage failed the declared gate.

03

Governance before deployment

Frozen V1 artifacts remain available for audit, but HTTP scoring is disabled. The public site now records the evidence instead of presenting current-player probabilities.
